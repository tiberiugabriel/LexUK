# Domain: consumers and e-commerce

Starting acts: acts-registry.md, section A (and I for corporate disclosures, accessibility). All verified live. If the business sells to EU consumers, also read domain-eu-interface.md.

## When it applies

When goods, digital content or services are sold to consumers (individuals acting wholly or mainly outside their trade, business, craft or profession). For pure B2B, most protections below do not apply, but general contract law, the Sale of Goods Act 1979, UCTA 1977 and some E-Commerce Regulations duties do (verify).

## Regulators and enforcement

- **CMA**: direct enforcement of consumer law under the DMCC Act (investigations, infringement notices, penalties); verify commencement and current guidance.
- **Trading Standards** (local authorities; in Scotland and NI their equivalents) and **CTSI**; guidance via Business Companion.
- **ASA** for advertising content (see domain-ip-competition-advertising.md).
- Consumers enforce their own rights in the county court (England and Wales), sheriff court (Scotland) or county court (NI).

## What to check

### 1. Mandatory information on the site
- trader identity, geographic address, email, registration details (Companies House number, registered office for companies), VAT number if registered: E-Commerce Regulations 2002 and the Names and Trading Disclosures Regulations 2015;
- information for service providers (Provision of Services Regulations 2009), including complaints handling;
- prices clear, including VAT and all mandatory charges.

### 2. Pre-contract information (distance contracts)
Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013 (verify the schedule of information):
- main characteristics, total price including taxes, delivery costs, payment and delivery arrangements;
- duration, termination conditions, auto-renewal;
- existence and conditions of the right to cancel, or why it is lost; who pays return costs;
- reminder of statutory rights for goods/digital content and any commercial guarantee or after-sales service;
- digital content: functionality, compatibility, interoperability;
- for marketplaces: whether the seller is a trader (check UK rules; the EU Omnibus requirement is EU law).

### 3. Order process
- the order button or similar makes clear that placing the order implies an obligation to pay (verify the required wording, e.g. "order with obligation to pay");
- confirmation of the contract on a durable medium;
- no pre-ticked boxes for additional payments (additional charges rules);
- proof of accepted terms and their version.

### 4. Right to cancel
- the cancellation period and when it starts (different for goods, services, digital content); verify the exact text;
- the model cancellation form and how cancellation can be exercised;
- exceptions (made-to-measure or personalised goods, perishable goods, sealed goods unsealed for health or hygiene reasons, digital content supplied with express consent and acknowledgement of loss of the right, etc.) and the exact conditions;
- services started during the cancellation period: express request, information, proportionate payment, loss of right after full performance;
- refund deadline, deduction for diminished value, who bears return cost;
- missing cancellation information: check the extension of the cancellation period.
- The EU "withdrawal button" (Directive 2023/2673) is EU law; it does not apply in GB unless the UK legislates. Check if the business sells to EU consumers.

### 5. Statutory rights and guarantees
- Consumer Rights Act 2015: goods must be of satisfactory quality, fit for purpose, as described; tiered remedies (short-term right to reject, repair or replacement, price reduction or final right to reject); burden of proof presumption for faults appearing within a set period (verify);
- digital content: separate rights and remedies, including damage to device or other digital content;
- services: reasonable care and skill, information given binding, repeat performance or price reduction;
- limitation period for claims: 6 years in England, Wales and NI; 5 years in Scotland (verify);
- commercial guarantees ("warranties"): must not restrict statutory rights; check the information requirements;
- defective products causing damage: Consumer Protection Act 1987 (strict liability).

### 6. Prices, fees and unfair commercial practices (DMCC Act Part 4)
- general prohibitions: misleading actions, misleading omissions, aggressive practices, contravening professional diligence; plus the list of practices banned in all circumstances (verify the schedule);
- **drip pricing**: mandatory fees must be included in the headline price (verify CMA guidance on what counts as mandatory and how to show delivery charges);
- reference pricing and discounts ("was/now", RRP comparisons): must not mislead; follow the pricing practices guidance; the UK has no statutory 30-day rule (the EU has);
- unit pricing: Price Marking Order (check the 2024 replacement and its application date);
- fake urgency, fake stock levels, untrue "X people viewing", hidden costs, hard-to-cancel subscriptions: misleading or aggressive practices;
- personalised or dynamic pricing: transparency to avoid misleading; data protection implications;
- environmental claims: CMA Green Claims Code and the general prohibitions.

### 7. Reviews
DMCC Act banned practices include fake consumer reviews and related conduct (verify the exact list):
- do not commission, submit or publish fake reviews or concealed incentivised reviews;
- take reasonable and proportionate steps to prevent and remove fake reviews (check CMA guidance on what is expected);
- do not present reviews in a misleading way (e.g. suppressing negative reviews).

### 8. Subscriptions and auto-renewal
- **DMCC subscription contracts regime** (pre-contract information, reminder notices, cooling-off for subscriptions, easy exit): **check commencement**. At compilation it was not in force; the government announced a January 2027 start after earlier delays, with regulations still pending. Until then, existing rules apply (Consumer Contracts Regulations, unfair terms, unfair practices: e.g. making cancellation hard can already be an unfair practice);
- clear information on duration, renewal, price after the trial;
- cancellation method not harder than sign-up (best practice now, likely a legal requirement under the new regime);
- consumer savings schemes (e.g. Christmas savings clubs): separate DMCC rules (check commencement).

### 9. Terms and conditions (unfair terms: CRA 2015 Part 2)
- fairness test and transparency requirement; prominence of price and main subject matter;
- grey list of potentially unfair terms (verify): broad exclusions, unilateral variation, disproportionate charges, auto-renewal traps, waiver of rights;
- terms excluding liability for death or personal injury from negligence or statutory rights are blacklisted (non-binding);
- CMA unfair terms guidance;
- versioning and archiving.

### 10. Cross-border and intra-UK
- consumers in Scotland and NI benefit from their local mandatory protections and courts; choice of English law and courts cannot remove them (verify);
- EU consumers: see domain-eu-interface.md (Rome I, Brussels I bis now apply from the EU side only);
- currency and language: no general obligation to provide Welsh for private businesses (check sector rules).

### 11. Dispute resolution
- ADR information: traders must tell consumers about ADR when a complaint cannot be resolved, in specified cases (verify ADR Regulations 2015 and DMCC Act Ch. 4 reforms and their commencement);
- **ODR: negative verification is mandatory.** The UK revoked the EU ODR link obligation after Brexit (according to the compiler) and the EU ODR platform itself has been closed (according to the compiler). Remove old ODR links unless verified otherwise;
- sector ombudsmen (e.g. Financial Ombudsman Service for regulated financial services).

### 12. Accessibility
- **Equality Act 2010**: service providers must make reasonable adjustments, anticipatory duty; websites and apps are services; no detailed technical standard in law, but WCAG is the practical benchmark;
- Public Sector Bodies Accessibility Regulations 2018 apply only to the public sector;
- **European Accessibility Act**: applies to UK businesses selling in-scope products or e-commerce services to EU consumers (check microenterprise exemption); see domain-eu-interface.md.

## Common pitfalls
- copying another shop's terms: obligations depend on product, role and processes;
- "no refunds" or "no returns" statements that contradict statutory rights or the right to cancel;
- calling statutory rights a "12-month warranty";
- delivery or booking fees appearing only at checkout (drip pricing);
- "was £X" prices that were never genuinely charged;
- incentivised or filtered reviews;
- citing the CPRs 2008 or the EU ODR platform;
- applying EU-only rules (30-day prior price, withdrawal button) to UK-only sales, or ignoring them for EU sales.

## Specific escalation
- disputes, Trading Standards or CMA contact, repeated complaints: consumer law solicitor;
- subscription model design ahead of the DMCC regime: consumer law solicitor;
- significant sales in the EU: lawyer in the main target Member State.
