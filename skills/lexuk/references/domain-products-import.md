# Domain: product safety, sector rules, imports, environment

Starting acts: acts-registry.md, section C. All verified live. For goods into Northern Ireland or the EU, also read domain-eu-interface.md.

## When it applies

When physical goods are sold to consumers (and, for some rules, to professionals), including via dropshipping or marketplaces.

## Regulators

OPSS (Office for Product Safety and Standards) and local Trading Standards in GB; district councils in NI; FSA/FSS for food; MHRA for medical devices; HSE for some workplace products; Environment Agency and devolved agencies for producer responsibility; HMRC and Border Force for customs.

## Step 1: which market, which rules

| Market | Main regime |
|---|---|
| Great Britain (England, Wales, Scotland) | UK law: GPSR 2005 + GB sector regulations; UKCA marking, with continued recognition of CE marking for many regimes (verify per regime and the end date, if any) |
| Northern Ireland | EU goods rules under the Windsor Framework: EU GPSR, CE marking (UKNI in some cases), EU sector rules |
| EU | EU GPSR, CE marking, EU responsible person, EU sector rules |

A seller in multiple markets may need dual compliance. Ask: `MISSING: TERRITORY`.

## Step 2: role in the supply chain

For each product or category:
- **manufacturer**: makes the product or markets it under its own name or brand;
- **importer**: first places a product from outside GB on the GB market (or from outside the EU on the EU/NI market);
- **distributor**: makes an already placed product available on the market;
- **UK responsible person / authorised representative** where a sector regime requires one (e.g. cosmetics);
- **fulfilment service provider**;
- **online marketplace** (new duties under the PRaM Act 2025: check).

Caution: own branding or substantial modification can turn a distributor into a manufacturer. A GB-based seller buying directly from an overseas manufacturer is usually the **importer** for GB: name and address on the product or packaging/documents (verify the rule and any easements).

## Step 3: identify sector rules BEFORE the general framework

GPSR 2005 is the safety net. Where sector (harmonised) legislation exists, it sets the specific requirements. Illustrative categories:
- electricals: Electrical Equipment (Safety) Regulations 2016, EMC 2016, RoHS 2012, WEEE, batteries;
- wireless devices: Radio Equipment Regulations 2017;
- connectable consumer products: PSTI Act 2022 security requirements (see domain-security.md);
- toys: Toys (Safety) Regulations 2011 (NI: EU toy rules, check the new EU toy regulation);
- cosmetics: UK cosmetics regime, UK responsible person, product information file, SCPN notification, labelling;
- food and supplements: FSA/FSS rules, food business registration with the local authority, labelling, allergens, health claims;
- textiles and footwear: labelling;
- PPE, machinery, construction products, pressure equipment, gas appliances;
- chemicals, biocides, detergents: UK REACH, GB CLP;
- medical devices (and products claiming medical effects): MHRA;
- e-bikes, e-scooters, lithium-ion batteries: priority area for new rules under the PRaM Act 2025 (check);
- furniture and furnishings (fire safety), nursery products, cosmetics-like products (check borderline);
- vehicles, parts, tyres; energy labelling and ecodesign.

If the product does not fit clearly: ORANGE and escalate to a product compliance specialist.

## Step 4: general framework (GB)

Check:
- the general safety requirement and duties of producers and distributors (GPSR 2005);
- warnings, traceability (name, address, product reference), monitoring and recall;
- notifying the authority when a product is found to be unsafe (verify the procedure via OPSS/Business Companion);
- the **Product Regulation and Metrology Act 2025**: enabling Act; check which regulations made under it are in force (online marketplace duties, new product safety rules, mirroring EU rules for some products);
- online listings: information duties are mostly via sector rules and consumer law in GB; in NI and the EU, GPSR requires specific information in the online offer.

## Step 5: imports (dropshipping, non-GB products)

- Who is the importer in customs declarations and who is the importer for product safety? The roles may differ.
- GB EORI for whoever lodges customs declarations; XI EORI for NI.
- Commodity code, UK Global Tariff duties, rules of origin (TCA preferences for EU-origin goods).
- Import VAT; low-value consignment rules (VAT charged at sale for goods up to the threshold; verify; see domain-tax.md).
- Prohibitions, restrictions, licences, sanctions.
- Border controls on certain goods (SPS for food, plants, animal products).
- Conformity documents from the supplier (UK/EU declaration of conformity, technical documentation, test reports). Without them you cannot substantiate conformity.

## Step 6: environment and producer responsibility (EPR)

Check obligations for:
- **packaging**: packaging EPR (data reporting, disposal fees, recycling labelling later) with thresholds based on turnover and tonnage; devolved regulations; check which obligations apply on the analysis date;
- **WEEE**: producer registration and distributor take-back obligations (including online sellers; check the distributor take-back scheme option);
- **batteries**: producer obligations;
- **deposit return schemes** for drinks containers (check start dates in each nation);
- marketplace obligations regarding sellers' EPR (verify);
- distance sales into EU states: EPR registration in each destination state.

## Common pitfalls
- "the supplier has CE" without documents: marking without declaration and documentation does not prove conformity;
- a dropshipper believing it is not the importer although no other GB operator exists in the chain;
- UKCA-only products sold into NI or the EU;
- missing UK responsible person for cosmetics, or UK address labelling where required;
- assuming the PRaM Act changed obligations already: check commenced regulations;
- ignoring WEEE take-back and packaging EPR thresholds;
- products described as "medical", "therapeutic", "antibacterial" without the regime that allows it.

## Specific escalation
- possibly dangerous product or consumer incident: product compliance specialist + solicitor, immediately;
- uncertain classification (supplements vs. medicines, cosmetics vs. medical devices): regulatory specialist in that field;
- imports at volume or GB → NI flows: customs adviser + tax adviser.
