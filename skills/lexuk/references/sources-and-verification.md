## 1. Core principle

Any conclusion with legal, tax or compliance effect rests on the official text **verified in the current conversation**. The content of this skill's files is static knowledge compiled at a given date. The "current" version from memory or from these files is not evidence.

The source lists below are **not exhaustive**. If an issue belongs to a regulator or register not listed here (a sector regulator, a devolved government, a professional body), find it and use it, applying the same hierarchy.

## 2. Source hierarchy

**Level 1: official normative text**
- legislation.gov.uk (Acts of the UK Parliament, Acts of the Scottish Parliament, Acts of Senedd Cymru, Northern Ireland legislation, statutory instruments, assimilated EU legislation as it stood in UK law)
- The Gazette (official public record; notices)
- For the EU nexus: Official Journal of the EU and EUR-Lex

**Level 2: case law**
- UK Supreme Court (binding across the UK on the points decided, subject to the separate legal systems)
- Court of Appeal (England and Wales), Court of Session (Scotland), Court of Appeal (Northern Ireland)
- High Court, Upper Tribunal, First-tier Tribunal (e.g. information rights appeals against the ICO)
- Competition Appeal Tribunal (competition, CMA decisions)
- CJEU: binding in the EU; in the UK, pre-2021 CJEU case law forms part of assimilated case law that higher courts may depart from; post-2020 CJEU case law is not binding in GB but may be persuasive. In Northern Ireland, CJEU case law on provisions applicable under the Windsor Framework remains relevant (verify).
- County court and sheriff court decisions: persuasive at most

Use the National Archives "Find Case Law" service, BAILII and court websites.

**Level 3: regulators (decisions, guidance, practice)**
- UK: CMA, ICO, HMRC, Ofcom, FCA, PSR, OPSS, Trading Standards (via CTSI and Business Companion), ASA/CAP (self-regulation but enforced with backing from Trading Standards and the CMA), Food Standards Agency / Food Standards Scotland, MHRA, Environment Agency and devolved equivalents, Companies House, Intellectual Property Office, NCSC (guidance, not a regulator)
- EU (for the EU nexus): European Commission, EDPB, national authorities of the relevant Member State

**Level 4: secondary sources**
- law firm briefings, Practical Law-type summaries, blogs, forums

Rules:
- Level 4 alone cannot contradict level 1. Use it only as a lead to the primary source.
- Regulator guidance is not law (except where statute gives it a status, e.g. statutory codes of practice that courts must take into account). Present it as "official guidance" and say what status it has.
- legislation.gov.uk may not yet show all effects of recent amending legislation ("changes to legislation" warning). When it does, check the amending act directly.

## 5. Verification protocol

For **each** act a conclusion relies on:

1. **Identify the exact act**: title, year, chapter or SI number, and which legislature made it (UK Parliament, Scottish Parliament, Senedd, NI Assembly). For EU acts: the CELEX number where possible.
2. **Open the official source** (level 1). If the fetch tool will not accept a URL directly, search for it first and open the official result.
3. **Confirm the status**: in force / partly in force / not yet commenced / prospectively amended / revoked or repealed.
4. **Check the extent** (E+W, S, NI) and whether there is a separate devolved instrument for the other jurisdictions.
5. **Check the "up to date" status**: legislation.gov.uk shows whether outstanding changes are not yet applied to the revised text. If so, open the amending act.
6. **Read the relevant section or regulation** in the current version. Do not cite numbers from memory without having seen them.
7. **Check commencement**: for recent Acts, find the commencement regulations (e.g. "Commencement No. X Regulations") and transitional provisions.
8. **For assimilated law** (former EU law kept in UK law): check whether it has been amended, revoked or restated by UK legislation since 2020, and whether the interpretation rules changed by the Retained EU Law (Revocation and Reform) Act 2023 matter (section 8 below).
9. **Check interpretation**: guidance from the competent regulator; relevant case law, if any. Note the status of the guidance (statutory or not).
10. **Check the EU nexus** (if any): the EU act on EUR-Lex, its application date and the transposition in the relevant Member State. See domain-eu-interface.md.
11. **Negative verification**: confirm the obligation has not been revoked, postponed, replaced or exists only in EU law (see pitfalls.md).
12. **Record** the result in the verification table in the answer (see response-format.md).

Whatever you did not complete is marked accordingly (section 10). No need to verify acts that do not affect the conclusion.

## 6. legislation.gov.uk specifics

- Search by title and year, or by type and number (e.g. "SI 2013/3134", "2015 c. 15").
- The "Latest available (Revised)" version is the consolidated text; the "Original (As enacted/made)" version is the text as passed.
- Read the "Changes to legislation" box: it lists amendments not yet incorporated.
- The "Extent" view shows where each provision applies; the "Commencement" information is in the "Timeline of changes" and the explanatory notes.
- Assimilated EU legislation (EU regulations as they stood at 31 December 2020) is published there under "Legislation originating from the EU". The EU's later versions are on EUR-Lex and may differ.

## 7. Bills, commencement and secondary legislation

- A Bill is not law. Check bills.parliament.uk for the stage and Royal Assent.
- After Royal Assent, many provisions need commencement regulations. Search legislation.gov.uk for "<Act name> (Commencement No. ...) Regulations".
- Many obligations are set out in secondary legislation (regulations) made under the Act, often after consultation. Check whether the regulations have been made and when they come into force.
- Government announcements of dates (press releases, consultation responses) are not law until the commencement instrument is made. Mark such dates `VERIFIED_NOT_COMMENCED` or `UNVERIFIED` accordingly.

## 8. Assimilated law (former retained EU law)

- EU law that applied in the UK on 31 December 2020 was kept as "retained EU law" and, from 1 January 2024, renamed "assimilated law" by the Retained EU Law (Revocation and Reform) Act 2023.
- The 2023 Act revoked some instruments, ended the principle of supremacy of EU law and general principles of EU law for GB law (subject to exceptions), and changed how courts may depart from retained EU case law. Verify the details on the text.
- Consequences: a UK regulation that implemented an EU directive keeps its UK text even if the EU directive later changed; and new EU acts after 2020 do not apply in GB unless the UK legislates.
- Northern Ireland: under the Windsor Framework, listed EU acts (mainly goods, customs, VAT on goods, SPS, electricity, state aid) continue to apply in NI, in their current EU version, subject to the framework's mechanisms. Check the Annexes and any "Stormont brake" developments.

## 9. EUR-Lex specifics

- CELEX number for legislation: `3` + year + type (`R` regulation, `L` directive, `D` decision) + 4-digit number. Example: GDPR = `32016R0679`.
- Address format: `https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:<CELEX number>`. If it does not work, search for the act.
- The act's page shows its status, consolidated versions, amending acts and national transposition measures notified by Member States.
- The UK version of an EU regulation (assimilated law on legislation.gov.uk) and the EU version on EUR-Lex may differ. Always say which one you are citing.

## 10. Verification statuses

Use these codes in the verification table (translate them into the user's language; see SKILL.md for Romanian):

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `VERIFIED_IN_FORCE` | text opened on the official source; in force and applicable |
| `VERIFIED_NOT_COMMENCED` | on the statute book, but the provision has not been commenced or the implementing regulations are not made |
| `VERIFIED_NOT_YET_APPLICABLE` | made/adopted, applies from a later fixed date |
| `VERIFIED_REPEALED` | repealed, revoked or replaced |
| `VERIFIED_RECENTLY_AMENDED` | in force, with recent or imminent amendments to flag |
| `EXTENT_UNVERIFIED` | text verified, but not whether it extends to the relevant UK jurisdiction |
| `TRANSPOSITION_UNVERIFIED` | EU directive verified, Member State transposition not (EU nexus) |
| `AUTHORITY_PRACTICE_UNVERIFIED` | text verified, regulator practice or guidance not |
| `CONFLICTING_SOURCES` | official or official-secondary sources disagree |
| `UNVERIFIED` | the official source could not be opened in this conversation |

Never use statuses like "probably in force".

## 11. Conflicts between sources

- Official text vs. secondary source: the official text prevails; flag the conflict.
- Revised text on legislation.gov.uk vs. a newer amending instrument: the amending instrument prevails from its commencement; flag that the revised text is not yet updated.
- UK law vs. EU law for the same activity (EU nexus): both may apply in parallel to different parts of the activity; neither overrides the other outside its territory. Analyse each separately and say which applies where.
- Northern Ireland: where the Windsor Framework applies, EU law applies there and prevails over inconsistent domestic provisions to the extent the framework requires (verify).
- Regulator guidance vs. text: present both; say the regulator will most likely apply its own guidance, but a court or tribunal may decide otherwise.
- Government announcement vs. commencement instrument: the instrument prevails; an announcement alone does not change the law.

## 12. When tools do not work

- No web access or search: say so explicitly at the start of the answer; all legal statements get `UNVERIFIED`; recommend verification on the sources above.
- An official site does not load: try searching; if it still fails, say what you could not open.
- Only secondary sources found: use them only as leads and mark the statement `UNVERIFIED` until confirmed on the primary source.
