# Domain: tax (VAT, invoicing, MTD, low-value imports, platform reporting)

Starting acts: acts-registry.md, section E. All verified live. For EU VAT (selling to EU consumers, Northern Ireland goods), also read domain-eu-interface.md.

## Special rule for this domain

UK tax figures and regimes change with each Budget and Finance Act. Therefore:
- **do not quote from memory** any rate, threshold, limit or deadline; take them only from the verified official source (legislation.gov.uk, GOV.UK, HMRC manuals and notices), with the version date;
- the default confidence level is more conservative than in other domains: a tax conclusion without live verification is RED;
- for any decision affecting registration, returns or payments, recommend confirmation by a chartered tax adviser or chartered accountant.

When storing or presenting a threshold, use the structure: value / applicable from / applicable until / source / verified on.

## What to establish

### 1. Tax status
- legal form: sole trader (Self Assessment, Income Tax, Class 4 NIC), partnership, Ltd (Corporation Tax), overseas entity;
- UK VAT registration: compulsory above the registration threshold (verify the current value and the rolling 12-month and forward-looking tests); voluntary registration; **overseas sellers** (no UK establishment) making UK supplies generally must register regardless of turnover (verify);
- VAT rates (standard, reduced, zero) for the goods or services (verify, e.g. food, children's clothing, books and e-publications);
- flat rate scheme, cash accounting, annual accounting eligibility (verify).

### 2. Place of supply
- goods sold within the UK: UK VAT;
- goods sold from the UK to EU consumers: zero-rated export from the UK (evidence required); EU import VAT due on arrival (IOSS or customer pays); see domain-eu-interface.md;
- **Northern Ireland**: goods follow EU VAT rules (XI), services follow UK rules; GB → NI and NI → EU movements have special rules;
- digital services to UK consumers: UK VAT at the customer location; overseas providers register in the UK;
- digital services from the UK to EU consumers: EU VAT in the customer's state; EU non-Union OSS (verify);
- B2B services: general rule is the customer's location (reverse charge); check exceptions;
- imports into the UK: import VAT and customs duty; postponed VAT accounting for VAT-registered importers.

### 3. Low-value imports and online marketplaces
- goods in consignments up to the UK low-value threshold (verify value): no import VAT at the border; instead, supply VAT is charged at the point of sale by the seller, or by the **online marketplace as deemed supplier** if sold through one (verify);
- online marketplaces are also deemed suppliers for goods located in the UK sold by overseas sellers to consumers (verify);
- marketplace record-keeping and joint and several liability for non-compliant sellers (verify);
- above the threshold: import VAT and duty on import (customer or seller as importer, depending on Incoterms; DDP vs. DAP has consumer-law implications).

### 4. Invoicing and Making Tax Digital
- VAT invoice content and when a full or simplified invoice is required;
- no general mandatory B2B e-invoicing in the UK at compilation (government plans announced: verify any adopted regime and its date);
- **MTD for VAT**: digital records and compatible software for VAT-registered businesses;
- **MTD for Income Tax**: sole traders and landlords above income thresholds, phased from April 2026 (verify thresholds and dates);
- record-keeping periods (verify).

### 5. Special situations
- vouchers (single-purpose vs. multi-purpose) and gift cards;
- account credit, loyalty points;
- refunds, chargebacks, bad debt relief, discounts: adjusting VAT;
- subscriptions, deposits and advance payments (tax point);
- delivery charges (follow the main supply, generally);
- digital services to consumers outside the UK.

### 6. Platform reporting to HMRC
- Platform Operators (Due Diligence and Reporting Requirements) Regulations 2023: digital platforms facilitating sale of goods, personal services, rental of property or transport must collect seller information, carry out due diligence and report to HMRC annually (verify scope, exclusions and deadlines);
- distinguish: the **platform's** reporting duty ≠ the **sellers'** own tax obligations;
- "trading allowance" and hobby vs. trading questions for individual sellers (verify).

### 7. Direct tax and other states
- trading income vs. hobby; trading allowance for small sole trader income (verify);
- Corporation Tax for companies; associated companies and rate bands (verify);
- permanent establishment in another state (staff, warehouse, fixed place): escalate;
- Romanian or other EU business with UK sales: UK VAT registration as overseas seller, possible UK direct tax exposure if a UK PE exists; double tax treaty.

## Common pitfalls
- quoting the VAT registration threshold or the £ import limit from memory;
- an overseas seller assuming the UK VAT threshold applies to it;
- charging UK VAT on goods sold through a marketplace that is the deemed supplier (double counting) or the reverse;
- exporting to EU consumers DAP without telling them they will pay import VAT and fees;
- treating Northern Ireland goods like GB goods for VAT;
- digital services to EU consumers taxed as UK supplies;
- incorrect VAT on vouchers;
- a marketplace ignoring platform reporting rules.

## Specific escalation
- registration, scheme choice, corrections, overseas seller status: chartered tax adviser or chartered accountant;
- HMRC enquiries, assessments, penalties: tax adviser + solicitor;
- GB/NI/EU structures, permanent establishment: international tax adviser.
