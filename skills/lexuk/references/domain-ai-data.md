# Domain: artificial intelligence and data access

Starting acts: acts-registry.md, sections D and G. All verified live.

## UK approach to AI

At compilation the UK had **no general AI statute** comparable to the EU AI Act. AI is governed by:
- existing law applied by sector regulators (ICO, CMA, FCA, Ofcom, ASA, MHRA, EHRC);
- **UK GDPR**, including the automated decision-making rules as amended by the DUAA (Arts. 22A–22D);
- consumer law (misleading practices, DMCC Act), equality law (Equality Act 2010: discrimination by algorithms), IP law (copyright in training data and outputs; check any reforms on text and data mining and AI-generated works);
- the government's principles-based framework and regulator guidance.

**Check live** whether a UK AI Bill or AI-specific regulations have been introduced or passed after compilation.

## EU AI Act (through the EU nexus)

The EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) can apply to UK businesses that:
- place AI systems or general-purpose AI models on the EU market;
- deploy AI systems in the EU (e.g. EU subsidiary);
- are providers or deployers outside the EU where the **output** of the AI system is used in the EU.

It applies in stages and some obligations were the subject of EU postponement proposals: **check what applies on the analysis date**. Roles (provider, deployer, importer, distributor, authorised representative) and risk categories (prohibited practices, high risk, transparency obligations such as chatbot disclosure and labelling of generated content, AI literacy).

## Typical e-commerce uses and what they trigger

| Use | UK check | EU check (if nexus) |
|---|---|---|
| customer chatbot | transparency to avoid misleading (DMCC); UK GDPR (what data reaches the provider) | AI Act transparency for chatbots |
| personalised recommendations | UK GDPR (profiling, objection); OSA for user-to-user platforms (check) | DSA recommender transparency for platforms |
| dynamic or personalised pricing | misleading practices; UK GDPR automated decisions; CMA interest in algorithmic pricing | Omnibus information on personalised prices |
| fraud detection, automatic order or account rejection | UK GDPR Arts. 22A–22D safeguards | GDPR Art. 22 |
| credit scoring, BNPL | FCA rules, Consumer Duty; UK GDPR | AI Act high risk (creditworthiness) |
| generated descriptions or images | misleading images (DMCC, CAP); IP | AI Act labelling of generated content |
| generated or summarised reviews | fake reviews ban (DMCC) | Omnibus, AI Act transparency |
| AI recruitment | Equality Act, UK GDPR automated decisions | AI Act high risk |
| content moderation | OSA duties; UK GDPR | DSA statements of reasons |

## The AI provider

- is submitted data used for training? (contract and settings)
- where is it processed? (transfers outside the UK; for EU data, outside the EEA)
- Art. 28 contract with the provider;
- provider documentation and acceptable use policies.

## Data access and smart data

- **DUAA 2025 Part 1** creates powers for "smart data" schemes (customer and business data sharing in specified sectors, like open banking): obligations arise only under regulations made for a sector; check which schemes exist.
- **Connected products**: no UK equivalent of the EU Data Act at compilation (verify). For connected products or cloud services offered in the EU, the EU Data Act applies (data access for users, cloud switching).
- **Digital verification services** framework under the DUAA (trust framework, register): relevant for identity and age verification providers.

## Common pitfalls
- a chatbot presented as a human;
- AI-generated product images that do not match the real product;
- sending customer data to an AI model without UK GDPR analysis;
- assuming "no UK AI Act" means no AI-related obligations;
- ignoring the EU AI Act when outputs are used in the EU;
- quoting AI Act application dates from memory.

## Specific escalation
- AI in hiring, credit, health, or significant automated decisions: specialist solicitor + DPO (RED);
- AI products offered in the EU: lawyer with AI Act practice;
- smart data or connected product schemes: solicitor.
