# Domain: intellectual property, competition, advertising

Starting acts: acts-registry.md, section H. All verified live.

## 1. Intellectual property

### What to check
- **rights in site materials**: product photos (own, licensed from the supplier, copied from other sites?), texts, fonts, music, video;
- **AI-generated content**: UK copyright has a special rule for computer-generated works (CDPA 1988 s. 9(3); check any reform), the AI provider's terms, risk of reproducing protected works; check the status of any text and data mining reform;
- **trade marks**: use of others' marks in ads, keywords, domain names, descriptions ("compatible with X" vs. suggesting an affiliation); is the user's mark registered at the UK IPO? EU trade marks no longer cover the UK (comparable UK marks were created for EU marks registered at 31 December 2020); for EU sales, an EUTM or national EU marks are needed;
- **passing off** (unregistered goodwill) in England and Wales; similar delict-based protection in Scotland;
- **counterfeit products** on marketplaces: notice and takedown, OSA where relevant, liability;
- **software**: who owns the code (CDPA s. 11: employer owns works made by employees in the course of employment; contractors own their work unless assigned in writing), open-source licences and compatibility with the business model;
- **agencies**: does the client contract assign or license rights? which parts remain the agency's?
- **designs**: UK registered designs, UK unregistered design right, supplementary unregistered design (check post-Brexit regime and reforms);
- **databases**: database right (UK-only after Brexit; EU database right no longer covers UK makers in the EU).

### Pitfalls
- product photos copied from the manufacturer or competitors without a licence;
- "I bought the theme or plugin, so I can do anything with it" (check the licence);
- freelancer agreements without a written assignment;
- assuming an EU trade mark protects the brand in the UK (or the reverse);
- assuming AI output is free of rights.

## 2. Competition

Relevant especially for marketplaces, distribution networks and supplier relationships. Regulator: CMA; appeals to the Competition Appeal Tribunal.

### What to check
- **resale price maintenance**: hardcore restriction (CMA has fined online RPM cases);
- **restrictions on online sales** imposed on distributors;
- **parity clauses**: wide parity is hardcore under the UK Vertical Agreements Block Exemption Order 2022 (verify); narrow parity treated differently;
- **exclusivity**;
- **self-preferencing** by platforms (Chapter II if dominant; DMCC Part 1 conduct requirements for SMS firms);
- **exchange of sensitive information** between competitors, including via pricing algorithms;
- **DMCC Act Part 1**: obligations apply only to firms with strategic market status, but businesses dealing with them (app stores, search, marketplaces) may benefit from conduct requirements.

## 3. Advertising and claims

Regulators: **ASA** (applies the CAP Code for non-broadcast advertising including websites and social media; BCAP for broadcast), with backstop from Trading Standards, the CMA and Ofcom.

### What to check
- **misleading advertising**: CAP Code + DMCC Act unfair practices (B2C) + Business Protection from Misleading Marketing Regulations 2008 (B2B, comparative advertising);
- **identifying ads**: influencer marketing must be obviously identifiable ("#ad"); CMA and ASA influencer guidance; affiliate links;
- **environmental claims**: CMA Green Claims Code, CAP rules; substantiation for "eco", "sustainable", "carbon neutral";
- **nutrition and health claims** (food, supplements): only authorised claims (GB register), conditions of use;
- **medical claims** for products that are not medicines or devices (MHRA, CAP);
- **cosmetics claims**: common criteria;
- **less healthy food (HFSS)** advertising restrictions online: check the application date and scope;
- **promotions and prize draws**: CAP rules; lotteries law (Gambling Act 2005) for prize draws requiring payment; free entry route;
- **gambling, alcohol, financial promotions, crypto**: special rules;
- **advertising to children**;
- **pricing claims** (see domain-consumer.md);
- **targeting** (see domain-cookies-marketing.md).

### Pitfalls
- influencers not labelling ads;
- "the best", "no. 1" without evidence;
- "100% natural", "eco", "sustainable" without substantiation;
- supplements promising to treat or prevent disease;
- paid prize draws that are unlawful lotteries.

## Specific escalation
- IP infringement letter or counterfeiting allegation: IP solicitor;
- exclusivity, parity or pricing clauses at volume, or CMA contact: competition solicitor;
- campaigns with health or environmental claims, ASA complaint: regulatory specialist + solicitor.
