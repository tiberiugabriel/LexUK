# Domain: platforms, marketplaces, intermediaries

Starting acts: acts-registry.md, section B. All verified live. Section references are indicative: confirm the numbering in the official text. If the platform has EU users, also read domain-eu-interface.md (DSA).

## When it applies

When the user stores or intermediates third-party content, products or services: marketplace, service or booking platform, platform with user-generated content (reviews, comments, chat, forums, profiles, listings), provider directory, classifieds platform.

## Step 1: Online Safety Act 2023 (Ofcom)

The UK has no DSA. The main UK platform regime is the Online Safety Act, focused on illegal content and protection of children.

Check scope:
- **user-to-user service**: users can generate, upload or share content that other users can encounter (includes reviews, comments, messaging, forums, many marketplaces with user listings or chat); check exemptions (e.g. "limited functionality services" where users can only comment on or review content published by the provider, internal business services, email/SMS only; verify the exact schedule);
- **search service**;
- **links with the UK**: significant number of UK users, UK as target market, or material risk of harm to UK users;
- **pornographic content providers**: separate duties (age assurance).

If in scope, check (via Ofcom codes and guidance; duties phased in):
- illegal content risk assessment and safety duties;
- children's access assessment and, if likely accessed by children, children's risk assessment and safety duties (highly effective age assurance for certain content);
- content reporting and complaints procedures;
- terms of service duties, record-keeping;
- categorised services (Category 1, 2A, 2B): additional duties; designation by Ofcom thresholds;
- Ofcom fees and notification (check thresholds).

Many small e-commerce sites with only "limited functionality" reviews may be exempt: verify the exemption conditions precisely. If not clear: ORANGE.

## Step 2: hosting liability (E-Commerce Regulations 2002)

- hosting defence: no actual knowledge of unlawful activity, and expeditious removal upon knowledge (verify regs 17–19 and case law);
- the defence may be lost if the platform plays an active role (optimising presentation, promoting offers): check case law (pre-2021 CJEU case law such as L'Oréal v eBay as assimilated case law; verify its status).

## Step 3: platform-to-business (P2B)

If the platform intermediates for business users (sellers, providers), check the assimilated P2B Regulation and the UK enforcement regulations:
- terms for business users: clarity, notice of changes;
- statement of reasons for restriction, suspension or termination;
- transparency of ranking parameters, including the influence of payments;
- differentiated treatment (when the platform also sells its own products);
- data access description;
- internal complaint-handling system and mediation (check the small enterprise exemption).

## Step 4: who is liable to the consumer

The central question: is the platform an intermediary or the seller?
- Who appears as seller at checkout, on the invoice and in communications?
- Does the platform set prices, take payment, handle delivery and returns?
- If the consumer reasonably believes the platform is the seller, the platform may be the trader for consumer law purposes (verify; also misleading practices under the DMCC Act if the status is not made clear).
- Inform buyers whether the seller is a trader or a private individual (good practice in the UK; legal requirement in the EU via Omnibus).

## Step 5: related domains triggered

- **Product safety**: current GB law (GPSR 2005) has limited marketplace-specific duties; the Product Regulation and Metrology Act 2025 enables new duties for online marketplaces: **check whether regulations have been made and commenced**. Product Safety Pledge (voluntary). In NI and the EU: EU GPSR marketplace duties. See domain-products-import.md.
- **VAT**: online marketplace deemed supplier for imported goods up to the low-value threshold and for goods sold by overseas sellers already in the UK; joint and several liability rules. See domain-tax.md.
- **Platform reporting to HMRC**: Platform Operators Regulations 2023 (sellers of goods, personal services, rental of property and transport). See domain-tax.md.
- **Payments**: if the platform collects and redistributes money. See domain-payments.md.
- **Data protection**: platform–seller roles (separate or joint controllers, or processor) and what data sellers see. See domain-gdpr.md.
- **Consumer law**: fake reviews duties under the DMCC Act apply to platforms publishing reviews. See domain-consumer.md.
- **Competition**: parity, exclusivity, self-preferencing, exchange of sensitive information; DMCC Part 1 only for SMS-designated firms. See domain-ip-competition-advertising.md.
- **EU users**: DSA (including legal representative), EU GPSR, DAC7 via EU platforms. See domain-eu-interface.md.

## Children

If the platform is accessible to children, check: OSA children's duties and age assurance, the ICO Age Appropriate Design Code (Children's Code), DUAA changes on children's data (verify), CAP rules on advertising to children, and the age of consent for information society services under UK law (do not quote the age from memory; verify the DPA 2018).

## Common pitfalls
- assuming "the UK has no DSA, so no platform rules": the OSA, P2B, hosting rules, DMCC review duties and VAT rules apply;
- assuming reviews or chat on an e-commerce site are always outside the OSA: check the limited functionality exemption carefully;
- considering yourself "just an intermediary" while controlling price, payment and the buying experience;
- not collecting and verifying seller data (VAT, HMRC reporting, product safety);
- not explaining ranking parameters or paid placements to business users.

## Specific escalation
- OSA scope and risk assessments, Ofcom contact: technology/regulatory solicitor;
- marketplace liability structure (seller – platform – buyer contracts): solicitor;
- collecting money for third parties: financial services solicitor;
- significant EU user base: lawyer with DSA practice.
