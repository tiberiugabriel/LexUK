# Drafting documents

## Principles

1. **The document describes reality, not a template.** Do not draft a privacy notice or terms without intake: role, products, data and money flows, third-party tools, targeted countries (UK only, or also the EU). A polished document that does not match practice creates risk instead of reducing it.
2. **Verify the acts before drafting.** Clauses that depend on deadlines, thresholds or wording required by law rest on the text verified live. Otherwise, mark them in the draft with `[TO VERIFY: ...]`.
3. **Do not copy other sites' documents.** Besides copyright issues, they describe other businesses.
4. **Clearly mark what the user must complete or decide**: `[COMPLETE: ...]`, `[DECISION: ...]`.
5. **Document language** is independent of the conversation language: documents for UK consumers are drafted in English (British spelling); versions for EU consumers follow the Member State's language requirements. Use UK statutory terminology ("cancel", "statutory rights", "controller").
6. **UK and EU versions.** If the business sells to both UK and EU consumers, decide with the user between one document with clearly separated UK and EU sections or two documents. Do not blend the regimes (e.g. cancellation vs. withdrawal, statutory rights vs. 2-year legal guarantee, ICO vs. EU authority).
7. **Jurisdiction clauses.** Governing law and jurisdiction clauses for consumers must not deprive consumers in Scotland, Northern Ireland or EU states of mandatory protections; flag for specialist validation.
8. **Versioning**: each document has an effective date and version number; keep an archive of previous versions.
9. **Human validation**: at the end, list the clauses that must be validated by a specialist.
10. **No em dash** in drafted documents (house style); use a colon, comma or a new sentence.

## What you can draft and what must be validated

| Document | Full draft? | Mandatory validation for |
|---|---|---|
| Terms and conditions (B2C) | yes, after intake | liability limits, governing law and jurisdiction, loss of the right to cancel, subscriptions, unilateral variation |
| Seller terms (marketplace) | yes, after intake | liability between parties, suspension and termination, ranking, payments, P2B rules, Online Safety Act terms of service duties |
| Terms of service for user-to-user features | yes, after intake | Online Safety Act requirements for terms (if in scope) |
| Privacy notice | yes, after the data map | international transfers, legitimate interests and recognised legitimate interests, special categories, automated decision-making, EU representative details |
| Cookie policy | yes, after the technical inventory | match with what actually runs on the site; reliance on PECR exemptions |
| Model cancellation form and cancellation information | yes | conformity with the model in the Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013 |
| Returns and refunds policy | yes | exceptions, costs, deadlines, statutory rights wording |
| Subscription terms and notices | draft | DMCC subscription regime: only once commenced and regulations made; mark `[TO VERIFY]` |
| Data processing agreement (Art. 28) | yes, based on the UK GDPR minimum terms | liability, audits, sub-processors, transfers (IDTA/Addendum) |
| Joint controller arrangement | draft | allocation of responsibilities |
| Record of processing activities | yes, from the data map | completeness |
| Breach response procedure | yes | actual roles and deadlines |
| Subject access request procedure | yes | verified deadlines and DUAA changes |
| Legitimate interests assessment | draft | the conclusion |
| DPIA | structure and draft | the conclusion; usually requires a DPO |
| Online Safety Act risk assessment | structure and draft | conclusions; Ofcom guidance |
| Accessibility statement | draft | conformity with applicable requirements (EAA for EU sales) |
| Modern Slavery statement | draft (if in scope) | board approval and content |
| B2B contracts (services, licence, IP assignment) | draft | everything, before signing |

Do not draft as final text: responses to regulators, breach notifications to the ICO, court documents, tax opinions, filings with HMRC or Companies House. You may prepare drafts or structures, clearly marked for validation.

## Minimum structures to check

### B2C terms and conditions (UK)
trader identity and trading disclosures (company name, number, registered office, VAT number if registered); contact details; contract formation (steps, moment); prices including VAT and all mandatory fees; delivery; right to cancel (or why it is lost) and procedure; statutory rights under the Consumer Rights Act 2015 stated without restricting them, and any commercial guarantee separately; liability (without unfair exclusions); complaints handling and ADR information where required (no EU ODR link); personal data (reference to the privacy notice); changes to the terms; governing law (without depriving consumers of mandatory protections in Scotland, NI or EU states); version and date.

### Privacy notice (UK GDPR)
controller identity and contact details (DPO and UK/EU representative, if any); for each purpose: the data, the lawful basis (including recognised legitimate interests if relied on), the retention period; recipients; international transfers and safeguards; rights and how to exercise them; the right to complain (to the controller first, then to the ICO, according to the DUAA complaints regime: verify); source of data not obtained from the person; automated decision-making and its safeguards; whether providing data is required; version and date.

### Cookie policy
what technologies are used; table: name, provider, purpose, duration, type, whether consent or an exemption applies; how consent is given and withdrawn; how to object to exempt analytics if offered; reference to the privacy notice; update date.

### Data processing agreement
subject matter, duration, nature and purpose; types of data and categories of data subjects; obligations and rights of the controller; the minimum terms of UK GDPR Art. 28 (documented instructions, confidentiality, security, sub-processors, assistance, deletion or return, audits); transfers; annex with technical and organisational measures; list of sub-processors.

## Always include at the end
- the list of verified sources and the verification date;
- the list of remaining `[TO VERIFY]`, `[COMPLETE]`, `[DECISION]` fields;
- clauses requiring specialist validation and the type of specialist;
- a recommendation to update when processes, providers or legislation change (several UK regimes are mid-reform).
